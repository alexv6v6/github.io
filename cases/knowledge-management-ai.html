Knowledge management with AI

Designing AI-enabled knowledge systems that connect documents, structured data, and organizational expertise through a unified information architecture.

Overview

Organizations generate knowledge continuously, but that knowledge is usually fragmented across documents, databases, procedures, and the experience of the people who operate the business.

This case study describes the design of a knowledge management system that combines semantic retrieval, structured data access, and AI-assisted reasoning to improve information access and decision support.

The objective was not simply to implement a RAG pipeline. The objective was to design a system capable of representing organizational knowledge in a way that made it easier to search, understand, and use.

The challenge

The organization’s knowledge existed in two different worlds:

  • unstructured information, including manuals, technical documents, reports, and procedural documentation;
  • structured information stored in relational databases and operational systems.

Traditional search tools were insufficient because users needed answers that combined document context with structured operational information.

The challenge was to create a unified knowledge access model.

My role

I designed the conceptual architecture of the knowledge system and the information flows that connected documents, databases, and AI models.

This involved:

  • structuring document collections,
  • designing metadata strategies,
  • defining retrieval workflows,
  • integrating semantic search with Text-to-SQL,
  • designing evaluation mechanisms,
  • building functional prototypes for knowledge retrieval.

The project focused on knowledge architecture as much as AI implementation.

The design approach

The system was designed around a simple principle:

Users should not need to know where information is stored in order to obtain an answer.

Instead of separating document search from database queries, the system analyzed the user’s intent and routed the request to the appropriate knowledge source.

This required modeling:

  • document structure,
  • semantic relationships,
  • metadata,
  • database schemas,
  • query intent,
  • answer composition.

Knowledge architecture

The architecture unified multiple knowledge sources through a common retrieval layer.

Documents Manuals, procedures, technical knowledge
→
Knowledge layer Semantic retrieval, metadata, and embeddings
→
AI reasoning RAG, Text-to-SQL, and response composition
→
Decision support Contextual answers and information retrieval

Figure 1. Unified knowledge architecture connecting unstructured documents, structured databases, and AI-assisted reasoning.

Design decisions

Knowledge before retrieval

The quality of retrieval depended primarily on document preparation and metadata design rather than model selection.

Hybrid retrieval

Semantic search and structured SQL retrieval were combined instead of treated as separate systems.

Evaluation-first development

Prompt quality, retrieval accuracy, latency, and response quality were measured systematically rather than assumed.

Specification-driven AI

AI components were designed around explicit specifications, evaluation criteria, and repeatable workflows.

The result

The resulting system enabled:

  • natural language access to organizational knowledge,
  • hybrid document and database retrieval,
  • improved information discovery,
  • reduced search effort,
  • more consistent knowledge access,
  • a foundation for AI-assisted knowledge management.

More importantly, the project demonstrated that AI systems are fundamentally information systems.

Their effectiveness depends on how knowledge is modeled, structured, evaluated, and maintained.

Design principle

Effective AI systems depend more on knowledge architecture than on model selection.

Retrieval quality, response quality, and long-term maintainability emerge from a well-designed information model rather than from AI technology alone.
