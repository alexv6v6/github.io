# Designing a regulatory calculation engine ## Translating complex regulatory rules into an information system ### Overview Regulated industries often depend on calculations that must be applied consistently across thousands of transactions, while remaining aligned with evolving legal and technical frameworks. This case study describes the design of a regulatory calculation engine that transformed a complex administrative calculation process into a structured and maintainable information system. The objective was not simply to automate a calculation. The objective was to model regulatory knowledge in a way that could be implemented consistently, validated reliably, and evolved over time. --- ## The challenge The calculation process was governed by a regulatory framework that contained multiple variables, conditional rules, technical parameters, and exceptional scenarios. The primary challenges included: * interpreting regulatory requirements, * identifying the variables that influenced the calculation, * defining the relationships between those variables, * handling exceptions, * ensuring consistency across different operational scenarios, * supporting future regulatory changes. The greatest complexity was not computational. It was conceptual. The regulation described the calculation in legal and technical language, while the software required explicit and structured business rules. --- ## My role My responsibility was to design the functional logic that would later be implemented in the information system. This involved: * analyzing the regulatory framework, * identifying calculation variables, * modeling business rules, * defining decision logic, * validating calculation scenarios, * collaborating with technical teams during implementation, * supporting the evolution of the model over multiple years. The calculation engine was initially represented through database-level business logic and later evolved toward a dedicated functional module. --- ## The design approach The first step was to separate the regulatory knowledge from the implementation technology. Instead of focusing on SQL, the analysis focused on understanding: * what the regulation required, * how users applied the rules, * which decisions were deterministic, * which scenarios required exceptions, * how the calculation should be validated. The calculation was decomposed into explicit business rules. Each rule was modeled independently before being integrated into the overall calculation process. This significantly reduced ambiguity during implementation. --- ## Information model The calculation engine required a structured representation of: * regulatory parameters, * operational variables, * user classifications, * technical conditions, * temporal conditions, * calculation outcomes, * validation states. The objective was to create a model where regulatory logic could be traced and audited. --- ## Design decisions Several design decisions proved particularly important. ### Explicit business rules Regulatory logic was documented as business rules rather than implicit implementation behavior. ### Separation of variables Calculation variables were identified independently of database implementation. ### Validation-first approach Representative calculation scenarios were validated before implementation. ### Evolution-oriented design The model was designed with the expectation that regulations would change. This reduced the long-term cost of maintaining the calculation engine. --- ## The result The resulting system provided: * consistent application of regulatory calculations, * reduced manual interpretation, * improved traceability, * easier validation, * greater maintainability, * a foundation for future functional evolution. More importantly, the project demonstrated that the central problem was not software development. It was knowledge modeling. --- ## Design principle **Complex regulatory calculations should be modeled as business rule systems rather than embedded directly into implementation logic.** When regulatory knowledge is modeled explicitly, software becomes easier to validate, maintain, and evolve.
