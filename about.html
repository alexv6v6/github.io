About

Designing information systems that transform complex business knowledge into implementable software solutions.

I believe that the most valuable asset in a software system is not the technology—it is the knowledge that the system represents.

I’m a Systems Engineer with a Master’s degree in Strategic Information Management, and for more than eleven years I have worked at the intersection of business, regulation, information, and technology.

My work has rarely been about choosing a programming language or optimizing infrastructure. Instead, it has focused on understanding how organizations think, make decisions, and operate, and then translating that knowledge into systems that people can actually use.

Throughout my career I have worked with multidisciplinary teams including legal specialists, technical experts, operational staff, and software developers. My role has consistently been to bridge those worlds: understanding complex business processes, modeling information, defining business rules, designing workflows, and creating functional specifications that can be implemented consistently.

Knowledge before technology

One insight has shaped the way I approach systems design.

A calculation remains the same regardless of the programming language. Whether it is implemented in Java, .NET, Python, or SQL, the underlying business logic does not change. The real challenge is understanding the rule, the process, and the meaning behind the calculation.

That is why I focus first on knowledge, then on technology.

In recent years I have been exploring how artificial intelligence can strengthen this approach. My work with RAG architectures, Text-to-SQL, knowledge management systems, and specification-driven development is not driven by interest in AI tools alone. It is driven by a broader question: how can organizations transform complex knowledge into systems that are easier to build, evolve, and use?

The case studies in this portfolio are not intended to showcase code. They are intended to document design decisions, modeling approaches, and problem-solving strategies. Most of them are based on real projects involving regulatory processes, information systems, and organizational knowledge, presented in a generalized form to respect confidentiality.

My goal is to build systems where knowledge is structured clearly, business rules are explicit, information models are coherent, and technology serves the problem rather than defining it.

What I care about

  • Business systems analysis
  • Functional solution design
  • Information and process modeling
  • Knowledge management
  • AI-enabled decision-support systems
  • Specification-driven software design

I enjoy working on problems that require understanding before implementation, because I believe that the quality of a system is determined long before the first line of code is written.
